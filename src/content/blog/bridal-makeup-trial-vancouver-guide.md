The bridal makeup trial, also called a preview session, is the closest thing to a dress rehearsal for your wedding day look. It is a private 2 to 2.5 hour session where you and your makeup artist design, test, and perfect the exact look you will wear on the day, before any of the pressure is on.

For Vancouver brides, the trial is not a luxury extra. It is the step that removes almost every "what if" from your wedding morning: will the makeup hold up through a humid August ceremony, will the lashes still feel right after six hours, will the foundation match your skin in both daylight and ballroom lighting. This guide covers when to book your bridal makeup trial in Vancouver, what to bring, how to prepare your skin, and the questions worth asking so the session actually earns its place in your planning calendar.

## 1. When to book your trial

Book your wedding date first, then your trial. At Vera Makeup Studio, wedding dates are confirmed with a non-refundable deposit and a signed agreement, and summer and fall weekends in Vancouver go first, often 6 to 9 months out.

The trial itself is best scheduled 2 to 3 months before the wedding. By then you have your dress, your venue, and a good idea of the day's timeline, which means the look you test is the look you keep. Booking any earlier risks designing around decisions you have not made yet. Booking later leaves no room to adjust if something needs to change.

One practical note: if you have an engagement shoot or maternity session coming up, a preview can do double duty. Book it close to that shoot and you walk away with a tested look plus professional photos of exactly how it will appear in front of a camera.

## 2. What to bring

A well-prepared trial starts before you sit in the chair. Bring:

- **Inspiration photos.** A few looks you love, and two or three you do not. Knowing what to avoid saves just as much time as knowing what to aim for.
- **Your veil, hair accessories, and earrings.** Jewelry changes how a look reads; your artist should design around the full picture.
- **A photo of your dress neckline** and straps, so the makeup extends correctly across your chest and shoulders.
- **Your glasses, or your contact case.** You need to see the finished look clearly, and the case lets you test how the makeup holds up with lenses in.
- **A list of your skincare products and any allergies.** The artist needs this before anything touches your skin.

## 3. Skin prep in the week before

The best trial prep is boring, and that is the point.

- No new skincare products in the final 7 to 10 days. Your skin's reaction to a brand-new serum is a gamble you do not need.
- No chemical peels, extractions, or heavy exfoliation the week of. Redness from a facial can take days to settle.
- Dermaplaning 3 to 5 days before the trial, if you do it at all, so any razor sensitivity fades.
- Hydrate and sleep normally. A late night before the trial shows up in the skin, and the whole point is seeing what your real skin looks like.
- Arrive with a clean, moisturized face. It saves the session time that is better spent on the actual look.

Skip the alcohol the night before, too. It is not a lecture, it is just that puffy, dehydrated skin is not the skin your makeup will need to work with on the wedding day.

## 4. What happens during the session

A bridal preview at Vera's studio runs 2 to 2.5 hours. Expect this shape:

- A consultation on your preferences, your dress, your venue's lighting, and how much you normally wear.
- Skin preparation and a base matched to your skin type.
- The full look applied, start to finish, the same way it will be on the day.
- Photos in natural light and phone flash, so you can judge how it translates to pictures.
- A wear-test conversation: how it feels after an hour, what you would tweak, what stays.

## 5. Questions worth asking

Come with questions and you will leave with more than a pretty look. Useful ones:

- Is the trial fee credited toward my wedding day booking if I proceed?
- What travel fees apply for my venue? (Vera works on-site across Vancouver and the Lower Mainland, with fees calculated by distance and venue accessibility.)
- Are false lashes included on the day? (For brides, premium lash application is part of the day-of package.)
- What time do you arrive on the morning of the wedding? (There is no early-morning fee for brides at Vera's studio.)
- What happens if I change my mind about the look after the trial?

## 6. After the trial

Take photos immediately, from several angles, in whatever light the room has. Write down what you loved and the one or two things you want changed, because in a month you will not remember the difference between "slightly more peach" and "slightly more rose."

Note how long the makeup lasted and how it felt at the end of the day. That information decides whether you need the 6-hour touch-up package or the 10-hour VIP one, and whether anything in the base needs adjusting for a long ceremony.

If a lot changed during the session, one more refinement visit is common and worth scheduling while the date is still far out. If you have not already, lock your wedding date with the deposit so the trial look has a day to live on.
